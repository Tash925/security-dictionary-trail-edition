THE SECURITY DICTIONARY: TRAIL EDITION — Concept lines
======================================================

One CONCEPT line per security term. Drop into the master template's CONCEPT
line. Each term maps to a findable (or doable) outdoor experience, chosen so a
child can physically act it out rather than memorize a definition.

IOC — Indicator of Compromise — a clue that someone or something was somewhere
they shouldn't be.
  Outdoor experience: find evidence something was here (footprints, a
  knocked-over pot, crumbs a bird left).

Alert — a warning that tells you to pay attention because something might need
looking at.
  Outdoor experience: notice things that grab your attention (a loud bird call,
  a dog barking, leaves rustling).

Firewall — a barrier that decides what is allowed through and what gets kept out.
  Outdoor experience: find barriers that filter (a fence, a gate, a hedge, a
  screen door).

Encryption — turning a message into a secret code so only someone with the key
can read it.
  Outdoor experience: invent a secret signal only you and a trusted person
  understand (the secret rule is the "key").

Phishing — something that looks friendly or inviting but is really a trick to
get you to trust it.
  Outdoor experience: notice something bright and tempting — feel the pull, but
  pause and don't go to it.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
Design note: terms split into "concrete" (IOC, Alert, Firewall) which map to a
findable object, and "abstract" (Encryption, Phishing) which need a do-it or
notice-it framing instead. Abstract terms need concept-matched examples in the
prompt or the small model drifts. Not every security term fits this format —
the ones chosen are the ones that translate to physical discovery.
