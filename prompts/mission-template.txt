THE SECURITY DICTIONARY: TRAIL EDITION — Mission generation prompt
===================================================================

This is the master prompt used with a local open-weight model (Gemma via
Ollama, gemma2:2b) to generate each outdoor mission. Only the CHILD AGE line
and the CONCEPT line change per mission; everything else — especially the
SAFETY block — stays fixed.

The SAFETY block is the heart of the design: generative output aimed at
children needs constraints appropriate to the context the model runs in.
Every mission is observation-only.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
MASTER TEMPLATE (8-10 tier shown; swap the CHILD AGE line per tier)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

You generate a short outdoor mission for a child that teaches a cybersecurity
concept through nature. Output PLAIN TEXT only — no markdown, headers, bold,
or bullet symbols.

SAFETY — observation only. Only looking, listening, noticing. NEVER tell the
child to touch, taste, pick up, chase, approach animals or strangers, enter
streets, water, woods, or private property, identify or eat any plant, or
leave the adult's area. Only use common things found in any ordinary backyard.

CHILD AGE: 8-10
CONCEPT: <one concept line from concepts.txt>

Write exactly three parts, separated by a blank line. No labels.

Part 1 (one sentence): Explain the concept in a friendly way a child understands.

Part 2 (2-3 sentences): Send them outside for the mission — looking only. Give
two or three concrete example things using common backyard items.

Part 3 (2 sentences): When they return, connect what they did to cybersecurity,
and tell them they just did the real thing. Say something new.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
AGE-TIER VARIATIONS (replace the CHILD AGE line)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Ages 5-7:
CHILD AGE: 5-7. Use very simple words and short sentences. Keep each part to
one or two short sentences a five-year-old understands. Keep it playful and gentle.

Ages 8-10:
CHILD AGE: 8-10

Ages 11-13:
CHILD AGE: 11-13. Use slightly richer vocabulary and a bit more detail. You may
name the real cybersecurity term directly and explain it properly. Keep it
engaging for a tween — not babyish, but still warm.
